### EU and UK

- **When:** you have 6 months from the US filing date to file abroad and keep the US date as your priority date (Paris
  Convention). File in the EU and UK only if EU/UK members or marketing will matter within 2 years; otherwise let the
  window pass and file later on your own date.
- **How:** either through the USPTO as a Madrid Protocol international application designating the EU and UK (one
  form, fees in Swiss francs: a basic fee of about CHF 653 plus the EU and UK individual fees), or directly at EUIPO
  (about EUR 850 for one class, EUR 900 for two) and the UKIPO (about GBP 170 for one class plus GBP 50 per extra
  class). Expect roughly **$1,800 to $2,500** in official fees for the word mark in the EU and UK, two classes.
  Re-check fees on the day.
- **Recommendation:** decide at month 5 after the US filing. File the word mark only, classes 9 and 42.

## 3. Copyright

**What is protected automatically (no filing):** copyright exists the moment original work is fixed: the code you wrote
or directed and edited, the written copy, the guides' human-written templates, the app's design, the help pages, the
brand docs, the Goose and logo drawings, and the human-made parts of the videos (editing, selection, arrangement,
scripts). It lasts your lifetime plus 70 years (or 95 years from publication for a company's works).

**The AI-made parts are the catch.** The US Copyright Office registers only human authorship. Its 2025 report and the
2025 *Thaler v. Perlmutter* appeal decision confirm that purely AI-generated material is not protected; work where a
human selected, arranged or modified AI output can be, for the human part. For STT that means:

| Asset | How it was made (from the repo history) | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| App code | Largely written with AI coding tools under your direction, with your review, choices and edits | Copyright covers the human contribution; in practice the code is protected more by **keeping it private** (trade secret) than by copyright |
| Goose mascot | "The existing outlined goose" predates the 2026-10-21 hand-off; origin not recorded | **Owner to confirm who drew it.** If you or a hired illustrator drew it, it's fully protectable (get a written assignment if someone else drew it). If it was AI-generated, protection is thin; the trademark filing then matters more |
| Luggage-tag logo | Chosen on the "STT Design Directions" canvas (an AI design tool), combining the Goose with a tag | Mixed; trademark is the main protection |
| Guides and phrases | AI-written, cached | Not protected as such; protect the *database* with contracts (terms §6 forbids scraping) |
| Ads | AI-generated people and voices (Veo, Speechify) edited with real app footage | The edit and the app footage are yours; the synthetic people aren't copyrightable |

**AI tool output ownership:** Anthropic's terms assign Claude's outputs to you; Google's generative-AI terms don't
claim ownership of what Gemini/Veo generates for you; Speechify's commercial rights depend on your plan (legal review
L34). None of them can give you a copyright the law doesn't recognise, which is why the trademark and secrecy steps
come first.

### Should you register?

Registration (US Copyright Office) is needed before you can sue for infringement of a US work, and if you register
before the infringement (or within 3 months of publication) you can claim statutory damages and attorney's fees.

- **IP11. Register the Goose drawing** as a work of visual art if a human drew it: Single Application $45 (one work,
  one author who is also the owner) or Standard $65 (the LLC as owner of a work made for hire or assigned). The
  Copyright Office has proposed raising these to $55 and $85 around mid-November 2026, so filing before then saves a
  little. Disclose and exclude any AI-generated parts on the form. Who: Owner (30 minutes online at copyright.gov).
- **IP12. Don't register the code now.** Registration deposits part of the source with the Office (redacted deposits
  are allowed for trade secrets), the AI-assisted parts would have to be disclaimed, and secrecy (IP15) protects it
  better. Revisit before any investment or sale.
- **IP13. Videos and guides:** no registration; not worth it.

### The public repo and its licence

**Today:** `github.com/mishamachine/sillytraveltool` is **public** with **no LICENSE file** (checked with `gh repo view`).

**What that means legally:** with no licence, ordinary copyright applies: nobody may copy, modify, publish or sell the
code without your permission. GitHub's terms do let anyone view the repo and fork it inside GitHub. In practice a public
repo gives away more than code:

- **Trade secrets can't stay secret in public.** The AI prompts (inside `generate-guide`, `generate-checklist`,
  `generate-phrases`, `goosey-discover`, `receive-booking-email/ingest.ts` and `_shared/itinerary.ts`),
  the trip-matching and email-parsing rules (`_shared/tripMatch.js`, `route.js`, `ingest.ts`), the free-plan limits and
  the cost model are readable by any competitor; once public, they can't be treated as trade secrets.
- **Security:** no secrets have been committed (audit), but the code shows attackers where every check lives.
- **Copying is easy and enforcement is slow**, even though copying is unlawful.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Make the repo private (recommended)** | Protects prompts, rules and business logic as trade secrets from now on; no licence question | GitHub Actions: private repos on the Free plan get 2,000 minutes a month; macOS minutes count 10× (one 18-minute iOS cloud run uses about 180), so roughly 8 to 10 iOS runs a month fit, Linux checks and Android runs are cheap. If that runs short: GitHub Pro $4/month (3,000 minutes) or keep the iOS workflow manual-only. What has already been public may have been copied; rewrite the most valuable prompts after switching |
| B. Stay public with an "All rights reserved" LICENSE | Free CI minutes | Prompts and rules stay exposed; the file only states what the law already says |
| C. Open-source licence (MIT, Apache, AGPL) | Community, credibility | Competitors may legally reuse your work (AGPL less so); wrong model for a paid consumer app |

- **IP15. Switch the repo to private** (Settings → General → Danger Zone → Change visibility). Check afterwards: the
  cloud iOS/Android workflows still run, secrets intact, any public links (e.g. raw.githubusercontent images in docs or
  store listings) still work. Who: Owner clicks; Claude checks the workflows.
- **IP16. Add `LICENSE`** with "Copyright (c) 2026 [LLC name]. All rights reserved. No licence is granted." either
  way, and a NOTICE pointing to the open-source notices file (legal review L37). Who: Claude.

## 4. Trade secrets and contracts

Trade-secret protection only exists if you take reasonable steps to keep the information secret.

**Keep private (list in `docs/TRADE_SECRETS.md`, a private file once the repo is private):**
1. AI prompts and few-shot examples for parsing, guides, HONK, packing and phrases.
2. Trip-matching, multi-leg and duplicate rules (`tripMatch.js`, `route.js`, `ingest.ts` heuristics).
3. Free-plan limits strategy, pricing experiments, conversion data and the cohort/cost model.
4. Abuse thresholds and kill-switch settings (public thresholds help abusers).
5. Member data, obviously; the cockpit and admin tools.
6. Unreleased product plans (`docs/*_PLAN.md`) and the agent operating model.

**Steps:**
- **IP17.** Make the repo private (IP15) and keep prompts in the repo but not in any public page, help text or
  `llms.txt` beyond a feature description. Who: Owner, Claude.
- **IP18. Contributor and contractor IP assignment.** Before anyone else touches code, art, copy or video (a freelancer,
  a friend, a designer), they sign a short agreement. Template below. Who: Owner.
- **IP19. AI tools:** use AI tools on accounts whose terms give you the output and don't train on your inputs (business
  or API terms where available); don't paste member data into consumer AI chats. Who: Owner.

### Template: contractor IP assignment and confidentiality (one page; attorney to adapt)

> **Agreement between [LLC name] ("Company") and [Name] ("Contractor"), dated [date].**
> 1. **Work.** Contractor will provide [description] ("Work") for [fee].
> 2. **Ownership.** Everything Contractor creates for the Company under this agreement, including code, designs,
>    artwork, text, audio and video, and all intellectual property rights in it, belongs to the Company. To the extent
>    any of it is not a "work made for hire", Contractor assigns all rights in it to the Company, and waives moral
>    rights to the extent the law allows. Contractor will sign any document reasonably needed to confirm this.
> 3. **Pre-existing material.** Contractor keeps what they owned before. If any of it is included in the Work,
>    Contractor grants the Company a permanent, worldwide, royalty-free licence to use, change and distribute it as
>    part of the Company's products.
> 4. **AI tools.** Contractor will tell the Company which AI tools were used to make the Work, will use only tools whose
>    terms let the Company own and use the output commercially, and won't enter Company confidential information or
>    customer data into tools that may train on it.
> 5. **Third-party material.** Contractor won't include material they don't have the right to use (including fonts,
>    music, images and open-source code under licences that require the Company to publish its source) without
>    written approval.
> 6. **Confidentiality.** Contractor keeps the Company's non-public information (code, prompts, plans, data, customer
>    information) confidential during and after the work and returns or deletes it at the end.
> 7. **Independent contractor.** Contractor is not an employee. Washington law governs.
> Signed: Company (by [name], Member) / Contractor.

## 5. Brand hygiene

- **IP20. One name, used the same way everywhere.** "Silly Travel Tool" in full first; "STT" afterwards; the mascot is
  "Goose" (no "the"), as BRAND.md says. Never "SillyTravelTool" in prose; "sillytraveltool.com" only as the address.

  > Play and other names belong to their owners.

  the App Store" using Apple's and Google's official badges and rules); no airline or hotel logos as decoration.
- **IP21. Domains:** turn on auto-renew and registrar lock for sillytraveltool.com at Hostinger (expires 2028-02-17;
